Toyota has done something South Africans have argued about for years: it has put a hybrid badge on the Land Cruiser 300. The **300 Hybrid** landed in local showrooms in **August 2026**, and it arrives wearing a big number — **341 kW** — which makes it, officially, the most powerful Land Cruiser Toyota has ever sold.

Before the diesel faithful reach for the pitchforks, it's worth understanding exactly what this thing is, what it costs, and — the question that actually matters — whether it's the 300 you should be buying, or whether the diesel most South Africans already choose is still the smarter call.

## The headline numbers

The hybrid uses Toyota's **3.5-litre twin-turbo V6 petrol** engine paired with a single electric motor and a small **1.9 kWh lithium-ion battery**, driving through the familiar **10-speed automatic**. Together they make:

- **341 kW** of power and **790 Nm** of torque
- **36 kW and 140 Nm more** than the non-hybrid 3.5-litre petrol
- A claimed **10 L/100km** combined (versus 11.6 for the non-hybrid petrol)
- **3,500 kg** braked towing capacity
- A claimed **40% sharper throttle response** than the 3.3-litre diesel

It comes in two grades:

| Model | Price |
|---|---|
| Land Cruiser 300 3.5T HEV **ZX** | **R2,287,700** |
| Land Cruiser 300 3.5T HEV **GR-Sport** | **R2,343,800** |

That takes the 300 range to six derivatives, and it slots the hybrid in right at the top of the price list.

## First, what kind of hybrid is this?

This is the part that trips people up, so let's be clear: **it is not a plug-in.** You never charge it from a wall.

The electric motor sits between the engine and the gearbox, fed by that small battery, and the system charges itself as you drive — recovering energy under braking and coasting, then feeding it back to add muscle and smooth out the power delivery. There's no real electric-only range. Think of it as a **performance-and-refinement hybrid**, not a fuel-sipping city hybrid. Its job is to make the 300 faster and more responsive, with a modest economy gain on the side.

That's why the number that jumped is *power*, not economy. At 341 kW this is a genuinely quick three-tonne SUV, and Toyota's claim of 40% sharper throttle response is the kind of thing you feel every day, not just on a spec sheet.

## The real question: hybrid petrol vs the diesel

Here's where South Africa is different from most markets. Overseas, "Land Cruiser 300" usually means the petrol. Here, the **3.3-litre V6 turbodiesel (225 kW / 700 Nm)** is the one that sells — because of how we actually use these vehicles: long distances, heavy loads, remote fuel, and an eye firmly on resale.

Stack them up honestly:

- **Economy:** the diesel wins. About **8.9 L/100km** claimed versus the hybrid's **10 L/100km** — and diesel is cheaper per litre. Over a big-kilometre touring life, that gap adds up.
- **Range and remote refuelling:** the diesel wins. Better range from a tank, and diesel is the fuel you'll reliably find in the small Karoo and bushveld dorpies where you least want a fuel worry.
- **Towing and load:** roughly a draw on paper (both 3,500 kg), but the diesel's fat low-down torque is what long-distance caravan and boat towers tend to prefer.
- **Outright performance and refinement:** the hybrid wins, comfortably. It's quicker, smoother and more responsive than anything else in the range.
- **Resale:** advantage diesel, for now. Diesel 300s have the strongest resale track record in SA. A petrol hybrid is new territory here, and how the used market values it is still an open question — worth thinking about if you change cars often.

So the honest verdict: if you're a tourer, a farmer, or anyone who racks up long rural kilometres and tows regularly, **the diesel is still the sensible Land Cruiser.** The hybrid is for the buyer who wants the flagship — the most powerful, most polished 300 — and does most of their driving on tar, with towing and outright ability as a bonus rather than the whole point.

## What you get for R2.3 million

Both hybrid grades are loaded. The **ZX** is the luxury-focused variant, with leather trim, heated and ventilated front seats, a heated steering wheel, adaptive LED headlights, a powered tailgate, fold-down third-row seats, a JBL sound system with rear-seat entertainment, and Toyota Safety Sense driver aids. The **GR-Sport** trades some of that polish for a more aggressive, off-road-flavoured look.

On the ownership side, the hybrid comes with a **3-year/100,000 km warranty**, a **9-service/90,000 km service plan**, and — importantly for anyone nervous about hybrid tech — an **8-year/160,000 km warranty on the HEV battery**. That battery cover matters: it's Toyota's answer to the "what happens when the hybrid bits get old and far from a dealer" question, and it takes some of the resale risk off the table.

## Price in context

At **R2,287,700 to R2,343,800**, the hybrid is a serious step up. For perspective, across our daily-updated listings the **median asking price for a used 300 Series is around R1,699,950**, with most of the market sitting well below the new hybrid's sticker. You're paying a real premium here — for brand-new metal, the newest tech, and that headline power figure.

## The verdict

The 300 Hybrid is an easy vehicle to admire and a harder one to *need*. It's the most powerful, most refined Land Cruiser money can buy in South Africa, and the 8-year battery warranty shows Toyota has thought about the long-tail worries. But it's also the most expensive 300, running a petrol drivetrain in a market that has always chosen diesel for very good, very local reasons.

Buy it if you want the flagship and drive mostly on tar. If you're heading north with a caravan behind you and a long way between fuel stops, the diesel is still the Cruiser that was built for the job.
